Data cleaning choices

I lowercased words so capitalization would not split their counts. Following the Week 04 tutorial, I removed punctuation so forms such as “commerce.” and “commerce!” would count together. I removed numbers and symbols to reduce interference from random numbers such as page count, and since our analysis is focusing on vocabulary, numbers aren’t as useful to us. I removed standard English stopwords and the tutorial’s historical stopwords to focus the comparison on content vocabulary rather than common grammatical words and to shorten the corpus in overall length.

Interpretation Questions

Do some documents share distinctive vocabulary?

Yes, documents A06785, A06789, and A06791 share words such as “moneys,” “exchange,” “realm,” and “commodities.” This suggests common disucssion of money and trade. Additionally, A32839 and A69858 share “east-india,” “company,” “charter,” and “tuns,” suggesting common discussion of the East India trade.

Are there documents whose “distinctiveness” seems driven by noise or formatting rather than content?

Yes several of the distinctive words in document A32827 seem driven by noise or formatting such as “2dly”, “pag”, and “etc”. Also document A06786 has words like “ll” and “ss” which seem driven by units of measure possibly. Generally, however, the documents seem driven primarly by content with a few documents exhibiting some distinction driven by noise or formatting.

Syntactic Difference

A06791 more often builds sentences by adding conditions and explanations to a main statement. A93819 more often joins actions or items together and adds details describing people, places, and things. A06791 uses more dependent clauses, while A93819 uses more coordination and noun modifiers. In A06791, ‘if the money were embased by allay’ adds a condition to the main statement.
